# Worked example: Cat6 Ethernet cable, 8-shot construction sequence

Sourced 2026-08-22 via ChatGPT (with web research/citations back to
CommScope, Leviton, Belden datasheets). Verbatim prompts below. Treat every
number in shot 8 as **placeholder syntax** (`[X mm]`) — fill it from the
real SKU's confirmed spec, never invent it, and drop the shot entirely if
the real datasheet doesn't have the number.

**Master rule applied to every shot below:** Photograph the actual
physical object as if this were a high-end engineering/product-commercial
shoot. Real polymer, copper, foil, paper, cardboard and machining
imperfections. Physically plausible gravity, contact, bending, compression
and reflections. No impossible geometry. One continuous camera movement per
shot. No object transformation. No material magically appearing or
disappearing. No floating components. No "perfect CGI cleanliness."

For maximum consistency across the sequence: generate shot 1 (or a still
reference) first, then use image-to-video / reference-based generation for
the rest so every shot is visibly the same physical cable.

## 1 — Shipping box/reel

8-second premium engineering product-film macro shot.

A factory-new Cat6 Ethernet cable shipping package sitting naturally on a
matte industrial workbench: the actual cardboard cable box or reel package
used for this SKU, with realistic corrugated-cardboard compression at
corners, tiny paper-fiber variation, folded seams, printed packaging
graphics and believable manufacturing tolerances. A section of the cable
exits naturally from the dispensing opening and rests against the box with
real gravity.

**Camera:** full-frame cinema camera, 65mm macro lens, ~0.8m camera
distance, shallow but believable depth of field, focus locked on the cable
exit point and front edge of the package. Slow 20cm lateral dolly left to
right, no zoom, no orbit. Subtle real-world micro-jitter from a stabilized
physical camera slider.

**Lighting:** large diffused overhead softbox, broad side key from
camera-left, weak white bounce from camera-right, controlled specular
highlight on the jacket, natural soft contact shadows underneath the box.
Neutral industrial studio environment, slightly warm cardboard against a
clean cool-grey background.

Show the weight and physicality of the packaging: the cardboard sits
firmly on the surface, the cable bends where it contacts the opening, no
floating edges, no impossible deformation.

Real macro product photography, high-resolution optical detail, restrained
cinematic contrast, physically accurate reflections, realistic lens
falloff, subtle surface dust and tiny manufacturing imperfections.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not a 3D render, not an illustration,
not a generic stock-video aesthetic, no floating objects, no morphing, no
melting geometry, no material changing, no impossible cable bends, no
exaggerated reflections, no plastic-looking cardboard, no fake depth of
field, no camera teleportation, no sudden focus jumps, no watermark, no
added captions, no added text unless existing packaging printing is
physically present, no invented logos.

**Audio:** quiet industrial room tone, subtle cardboard movement and faint
cable friction only.

## 2 — Full coiled cable

8-second photorealistic engineering-commercial product shot of the
complete Cat6 cable coil/reel.

The same exact cable from shot 1, now presented as a full continuous coil

gloss level, diameter and surface texture established previously.
Individual loops press naturally against one another; inner loops sit
slightly deeper and darker; outer loops catch slightly stronger

**Camera:** full-frame cinema camera, 90mm macro lens, camera positioned

Show slight variations in loop spacing and compression. The cable must not
become geometrically perfect.

**Lighting:** large overhead diffusion, narrow rim light grazing across
the jacket to reveal cylindrical curvature, soft frontal fill, dark but
clean background. Realistic specular reflection moving subtly along the
jacket as the camera travels.

Macro optical realism: visible extrusion texture, faint longitudinal
surface variation, tiny jacket imperfections, realistic rubber/polymer
sheen, slight dust accumulation in protected spaces between loops.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not 3D render, not synthetic product
visualization, no perfect mathematical coil, no floating cable, no
self-intersection, no cable duplication, no morphing, no liquefying, no
impossible bending stiffness, no changing jacket color, no fake
reflections, no excessive bloom, no excessive bokeh, no watermark, no
text, no logos added by generation.

**Audio:** almost silent studio ambience, extremely subtle polymer
friction between touching loops.

## 3 — Outer jacket macro

8-second extreme macro engineering photography of the Cat6 outer jacket.

Fill most of the frame with the same cable jacket. The cylindrical polymer

jacket. Weak fill from opposite side. Black flags around the setup for
natural contrast. No artificially glowing edges.

Reveal the jacket as a real continuous polymer extrusion, with light
physically wrapping around the cylinder.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not a 3D render, not smooth
computer-generated plastic, no procedural texture appearance, no perfect

melting, no rubber deformation without physical force, no fake engraved

## 4 — Shielding layer exposed

**Use this only for an actually shielded SKU (F/UTP, U/FTP, S/FTP). Do NOT
run it for U/UTP** — plain Cat6 U/UTP has four unshielded pairs and no
metallic layer at all; shipping this shot for that SKU is a fabrication.

8-second extreme macro technical product-film shot revealing the genuine
shielding layer of the same Cat6 cable.

The outer jacket has been physically stripped back at one end, exposing
the cable's actual shielding construction according to the SKU
specification: [F/UTP foil OR U/FTP individual pair foils OR S/FTP foil
plus braided shield]. The exposed layer must have physically correct
construction and material behavior.

For foil shielding: thin metallic foil with slightly wrinkled edges,
realistic overlap, tiny crease lines and non-uniform reflective behavior.
For braided shield: fine individual copper strands crossing over one
another with believable spacing, slight variation in tension, natural
metallic highlights and real braid geometry.

The stripped jacket edge remains physically attached to the cable. No
components float.

**Camera:** 100mm macro lens, ~1:1 reproduction, low 20-degree angle
relative to the cable axis. Slow controlled 8cm dolly toward the exposed
shield. Focus begins on the jacket edge, then transitions onto the
metallic shield.

**Lighting:** large soft overhead source plus narrow grazing rim light
designed to reveal foil wrinkles or individual braid strands.
Polarizing-style controlled reflections, no mirror-like chrome appearance.

Make the material behave like real thin metal: reflections move with
camera position; creases create irregular highlights; shield does not
glow.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not 3D render, not a generic "metal
sleeve," no invented shield type, no floating foil, no infinitely smooth
metallic surface, no morphing, no shield appearing from nowhere, no
melting, no impossible braid geometry, no chrome-plated look, no
holographic material, no watermark, no generated technical labels or text.

**Critical accuracy instruction:** preserve the exact shield architecture
of the real SKU; if the referenced product is U/UTP, show NO metallic
shielding layer.

## 5 — Twisted pairs exposed

8-second scientific macro product-film shot of the Cat6 cable core with
the outer jacket removed.

Reveal four authentic twisted pairs: 8 individual insulated conductors
arranged as four paired helices, maintaining realistic Cat6 construction.
Use the exact color code of the actual cable: white/blue + blue,
white/orange + orange, white/green + green, white/brown + brown. The pair
twist rates must look physically manufactured rather than mathematically
identical.

If the actual SKU includes a central separator/isolator, show that exact
component. Do not invent one if absent.

The conductors remain mechanically organized as a cable core. They do not
float apart.

**Camera:** 90mm or 100mm macro lens, close focus, camera aligned ~30
degrees to the cable axis. Slow 6cm forward dolly with a tiny 5-degree
lateral arc. Rack focus from the near blue pair to the orange and green
pairs behind it.

**Lighting:** soft overhead diffusion plus a narrow side light grazing
across the insulation ridges, revealing each helix. Controlled fill keeps
the rear pairs visible without flattening the depth.

The insulation should show subtle extrusion texture and realistic color
variation. White conductors are slightly warmer than pure digital white.
The copper should remain mostly hidden beneath the insulation.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not 3D render, no generic four-color
wires, no perfectly synchronized twists, no floating conductors, no
impossible intersections, no pair colors changing, no invented separator,
no morphing, no geometry popping, no glowing wires, no holographic
circuitry aesthetic, no excessive sharpness, no watermark, no added text.

**Audio:** faint dry movement of polymer insulation and subtle handling
sound, no electrical hum.

## 6 — Single conductor macro

8-second ultra-macro engineering photography of one individual Cat6
conductor.

Isolate one conductor from the exact cable construction. Reveal the
conductor as solid bare copper under its specified insulation, unless the
real SKU uses stranded conductors. Use the actual SKU construction, not a
generic Ethernet wire.

The insulated conductor rests naturally against a neutral technical
surface. A short stripped section reveals the copper underneath. The
copper is warm metallic orange-red with realistic microscopic
machining/drawing marks, tiny surface variations and anisotropic
reflections. The insulation edge is clean but not digitally perfect.

**Camera:** 100mm macro lens with extension-tube look, ~2:1 magnification,
extremely shallow depth of field. Slow 3–4cm lateral slider move. Focus
transitions from the insulation boundary to the exposed copper surface.

**Lighting:** enormous soft source from above-left, tiny hard rim source
at a shallow angle to reveal copper texture, controlled black negative
fill on the opposite side. Copper reflection must change naturally with
camera movement.

Show actual solid copper geometry rather than a strand bundle where the
SKU specifies solid conductor.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not 3D render, no generic orange wire,
no gold wire, no copper liquid, no glowing metal, no perfect mirror
finish, no floating wire, no morphing, no changing gauge, no invented
strands, no impossible cross-section, no watermark, no text overlay.

*Real Cat6 conductor gauge varies by product — Leviton lists 23 AWG bare
copper for one Cat6 U/UTP product; Belden lists both 23 AWG and 24 AWG
across different constructions. Use the real SKU datasheet's number, not
a generic "Cat6 wire" assumption.*

## 7 — Clean cross-section

8-second precision scientific-product macro shot of a mechanically clean
transverse cross-section through the exact Cat6 cable.

Show a perfectly perpendicular cut through the real cable, revealing the
true layer stack in one frame.

From outside inward, reproduce **only the layers that actually exist on
the SKU**: outer jacket → shielding layer if present → separator/isolator
if present → four twisted pairs → individual polymer insulation → copper
conductors.

The cut face is physically plausible: polymer edges slightly compressed by
the cutting process, copper faces cleanly exposed, insulation boundaries
continuous, shielding foil showing realistic thickness and torn/creased
edge behavior if the actual shield is foil.

No exploded-view spacing. Every layer remains in its actual physical
relationship.

**Camera:** 100mm macro lens, optical axis exactly perpendicular to the
cut face, camera locked off on a precision tripod. No orbit. Extremely
subtle 1–2cm forward push over the entire shot. Deep enough focus that the
entire cross-section remains readable.

**Lighting:** soft circular key source above camera, secondary side fill,
microscopic grazing light from the upper-right to reveal the cut surfaces.
Neutral grey background. High microcontrast without over-sharpening.

The resulting image should look like a frame from a real
telecommunications cable laboratory/product photography session, not a
textbook 3D visualization.

**Negative constraints:** not CGI, not 3D render, not exploded diagram, no
detached layers, no floating wires, no perfectly mathematically symmetric
pairs, no impossible concentric geometry, no morphing, no layer creation,
no disappearing material, no translucent jacket, no glowing copper, no
watermark, no text, no diagram graphics.

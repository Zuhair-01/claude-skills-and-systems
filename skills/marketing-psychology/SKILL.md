## When invoked, run this pass

### 0. Starving-crowd check (Halbert) — before anything else
Is there already a crowd hungry for this, with money, that you can name and locate — or are you trying to write your way around an indifferent market? Market beats offer beats copy, in that order. If the crowd isn't starving, stop and fix positioning/market selection (route to `taste-skill`/Dunford, same escape hatch as the hard rules below) — no copy step fixes this. Full framework: `Second_Brain/Workflow/30-Resources/Curriculum/Gary_Halbert_Framework.md`.

### 1. Diagnose awareness stage (Schwartz) — sets everything
Unaware → Problem-aware → Solution-aware → Product-aware → Most-aware.
The hook must meet the reader where they are. Most-aware wants a deal; unaware needs a story/hook first.
Mismatch here is the #1 reason copy fails.

### 1b. Diagnose market sophistication stage (Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*) — separate axis from awareness
Awareness = what this *individual reader* knows. Sophistication = how many competing claims the *whole market* has already absorbed — a market can be Most-Aware individually and still be late-sophistication collectively.
1. **First in market** → state the plain claim, no embellishment.
2. **Competitors match the claim** → escalate the claim (bigger/faster/more).
3. **Claim escalation exhausted, market is claim-skeptical** → stop claiming outcome, claim a **mechanism** ("our unique X method") instead.
4. **Mechanism claims exhausted** → dramatize the mechanism, or shift to identification (sell who they become).
5. **Total saturation** → only a new identification/niche/angle moves them; copy alone can't fix this, it's a positioning problem (route to `taste-skill`/Dunford, same as the hard-rules escape hatch below).
Diagnostic: plain benefit lands → stage 1-2. Gets an internal "yeah, everyone says that" → stage 3, go find/name a mechanism. A good mechanism claim *still* doesn't move them → stage 4-5. Full stage breakdown + Ostazi/Alwazour/Kyros stage calls: `Second_Brain/Workflow/30-Resources/Curriculum/Breakthrough_Advertising_Schwartz.md`.

### 1c. Check all three forces, not just awareness (Schwartz)
A weak ad usually fails at exactly one of these — diagnose which before rewriting anything:
- **Desire** — the raw want/fear already in them (never created, only channeled/gathered/focused/intensified — see the doc above).
- **Identification** — do they see themselves as *the kind of person* this is for? Can fail even when desire is strong.
- **Belief** — do they believe *this mechanism, from this source* delivers it? Separate from wanting and from identifying.

### 2. Nail the value equation (Hormozi) — audit the offer
```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort)
```
Push each lever: bigger true outcome · more proof/guarantee · faster first result · less work for them.
Test: "would the target feel stupid saying no?"

### 3. Answer the 5 objections before they're spoken
time · money · "won't work for me" · trust · "not now". Each needs a specific counter in the copy.

### 3b. Subject/envelope line survives on its own (Halbert's A-pile test)
Subject line, thumbnail, or preview text is a separate battle from the body — won or lost before the reader ever sees the offer. Audit it independently of body copy quality. And keep every piece to exactly ONE ask: competing CTAs don't get prioritized, they get ignored.

### 4. Apply persuasion principles (Cialdini's 7) — pick the 2–3 that fit, don't spray
reciprocity · commitment/consistency · social proof · authority · liking (founder visible) · scarcity (REAL only) · **unity** (shared identity / in-group — added in *Pre-Suasion* 2016; teach 7, not the old 6).
Each has a **triggering condition** — that's *when* it bites. Match the lever to the moment:
- social proof → strongest under **uncertainty** + from **similar** others (a testimonial from someone in the reader's exact situation > a celebrity or a raw count).
- authority → symbols work too (titles, credentials shown early), but a real named expert beats "studies show".
- scarcity → only fires if the constraint is **verifiable**; manufactured scarcity that gets noticed inverts the effect.
- **foot-in-the-door** (small ask → real ask, rides *consistency*) vs **door-in-the-face** (large ask refused → real smaller ask, rides *reciprocity*): both are about the *sequence*, not the final request.

### 5. Apply behavioral levers — but only the ones that survived replication
Robust, safe to build on: **anchoring** (arbitrary first number pulls estimates, 45+ yrs) · **loss aversion / framing** ("$1/day" not "$30/mo"; "90% keep it" not "10% churn"; a loss stings ~2×) · **availability** (vivid = feels more likely) · **decoy** · **cognitive load** (one primary CTA; delete options/fields/words) · **Zeigarnik** (progress bars, open loops) · **peak-end** (design the aha + the receipt/offboarding) · **hyperbolic discounting** (speed to first result).

**Do NOT cite these as settled** — they failed or badly weakened in replication, and referencing them as fact damages your credibility with anyone informed:
- **ego depletion** ("willpower runs out") — 23-lab RRR found d≈0.04.
- **social / behavioral priming** (money cues, cleanliness cues changing behaviour) — largely failed to replicate.
- **power posing** physiology (hormone/risk changes) — repudiated by a co-author.
- **most "nudge" field effects** — real but small and context-dependent; don't promise the headline number.
- any **recycled percentage** ("color boosts conversion 80%", "8-second attention span", "55% of communication is body language") — trace it to a primary source or drop it.
(Source: the Learning App research base — `Second_Brain/Workflow/10 - Projects/Learning_App/Research_Compendium/` Parts 2, 17, 24, 41; distilled decks `how-to-think`, `learning-mythbusters`.)

### 6. Structure the copy (pick by length)
PAS (short, problem-aware) · AIDA (versatile) · BAB (transformation) · 4Ps (ads/LP) · PASTOR (long sales page) · FAB (translate every feature→benefit).
Headline: **gather → focus → intensify** (Schwartz) — name the desire already there in the reader's own words, focus it on your product/mechanism, then intensify with a specific detail, in that order; intensifying before gathering the right desire reads loud and empty. Pick ONE headline job — direct promise, curiosity, news, how-to, question, command, reason-why, or warning — matched to the market's sophistication stage (1b), never mixed. 4 U's (Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific) still governs the wording. Lead (first 2-3 sentences): confirm the reader is the right person/problem in their own internal language *before* any product mention — pitching too early loses an identification-failure reader who'd have converted otherwise (1c). Ogilvy: headline is 80% of the work; cut adjectives, add a specific number/fact. Proof type must match sophistication stage: bare claim (stage 1-2) → mechanism explanation (stage 3) → specific checkable numbers/third-party proof (stage 4) → social/identity proof (stage 5). Full breakdown: `Second_Brain/Workflow/30-Resources/Curriculum/Breakthrough_Advertising_Schwartz.md`.

### 7. Map benefit to a primal driver (LIFEFORCE 8)
survival/health · food/drink · freedom from fear/pain · companionship · comfort · status/winning · protecting loved ones · social approval.
Fast practical subset (Halbert's 6 hot buttons, same territory, quicker to apply mid-draft): greed · fear · guilt · exclusivity · salvation/redemption · flattery. Each must still pass the integrity test below — a real risk/regret, never a manufactured one.

### 8. Voice, swipe, and test (Halbert)
Write like one real person to one real reader — contractions, short sentences, never "Dear valued customer" plural-corporate voice. Check the vault Style Library / prior winning pieces for this brand before writing from a blank page. Any outreach send (`wa-campaigns`, cold-email, ads) defaults to a small test tranche before a full send — don't guess at scale.

## Output format
0. Starving-crowd verdict (Halbert) — is the market hungry, yes/no and why.
1. Awareness stage + the mismatch you found.
2. Offer audit against the value equation (4 levers, what's weak).
3. Rewritten hook (3 variants).
4. Rewritten body using the chosen framework.
5. The 2–3 persuasion principles applied and where.
6. One CTA, with the reason-to-act-now.
7. Legends QA pass (Hopkins/Caples/Ogilvy/Sugarman/Bencivenga checklist) — flag anything that fails the disbelief check or looks like a belief/proof gap rather than a desire gap.

## Hard rules
- Never fake scarcity/urgency/social proof. It converts once and destroys trust + deliverability forever.
- Specificity beats adjectives. "3,412 founders" not "thousands of happy customers".
- Emotion sells, logic justifies — give them both, in that order.
- If the positioning is unclear, stop and fix that first (route to `taste-skill`/curriculum §5B Dunford). Copy can't rescue bad positioning.
- **The integrity test** (Hassan's Influence Continuum): does this copy/flow *increase or decrease* the reader's authentic connection to (a) their own critical thinking, (b) other people, (c) outside information? Increase = persuasion. Decrease = manipulation — and the second one is a business risk, not just an ethics one. Concretely, that rules out: pathologising doubt ("you have a scarcity mindset"), pressure that only works if they *don't* sleep on it or ask someone, and roach-motel opt-out friction.
- Don't cite a debunked effect to sound smart (see step 5). "Studies show" without a traceable study is worse than no claim.

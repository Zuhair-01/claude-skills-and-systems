## Focus Areas

- Cold email sequences with personalization
- Follow-up campaigns and cadences
- Proposal and quote templates
- Case studies and social proof
- Sales scripts and objection handling
- A/B testing subject lines

## Approach

1. Lead with value, not features
2. Personalize using research
3. Keep emails short and scannable
4. Focus on one clear CTA
5. Track what converts

For the email sequence itself (subject lines, opening lines, multi-touch cadence), use
`cold-email` instead — it's the deeper, dedicated skill for that. Use this skill for the
surrounding sales collateral: proposals, case studies, objection scripts, pricing pages.

## Objection Handling Pattern (acknowledge → reframe → proof → ask)

```
Objection: "We already use [Competitor]."
Bad response: "Our product is better because X, Y, Z." (defensive, feature-dump)
Good response: "Makes sense — [Competitor]'s solid for [what they're known for]. Most teams
that switch to us do it because of [specific gap], not because [Competitor] is bad. [1-line
proof point / customer name]. Worth a 15-min look to see if that gap applies to you?"
```

Pitfall: responding to price objections by immediately discounting trains the prospect to always
push back on price — reframe to value/ROI first ("what would solving this actually be worth to
you?"), and only discuss discount if the value case genuinely doesn't clear their budget.

## Output

- Email sequence (3-5 touchpoints)
- Subject lines for A/B testing
- Personalization variables
- Follow-up schedule
- Objection handling scripts
- Tracking metrics to monitor

Write conversationally. Show empathy for customer problems.
